### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Introduction – The Power of God’s Word</span>

God’s Word created the world. The same Word still brings life to us through the Holy Spirit.

In this message we look at the Ten Commandments given to Israel in Exodus 20. These commandments have influenced the laws of many nations because they protect human relationships. But their deeper purpose is to show us what God expects and to reveal our need for His power.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ The First Four Commandments – Relationship with God</span>

1. **You shall have no other gods before Me** (Exodus 20:3)  
2. **You shall not make any idol or likeness** (Exodus 20:4–6)  
3. **You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain** (Exodus 20:7)  
4. **Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy** (Exodus 20:8–11)

These call us to worship the true God, refuse idols, honour His name, and set apart time for Him.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Commandments Five to Nine – Relationship with Others</span>

5. Honour your father and mother (Exodus 20:12)  
6. You shall not murder (Exodus 20:13)  
7. You shall not commit adultery (Exodus 20:14)  
8. You shall not steal (Exodus 20:15)  
9. You shall not bear false witness against your neighbour (Exodus 20:16)

These deal with outward actions. A person can keep them to a large degree by living a morally upright life that others can observe. They can produce a good external testimony.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ The Tenth Commandment – The Heart</span>

> <span style="color:#276749;">“You shall not covet your neighbour’s house; you shall not covet your neighbour’s wife or his male servant or his female servant or his ox or his donkey or anything that belongs to your neighbour.”</span>  
> <span style="color:#276749;">— Exodus 20:17</span>

This commandment is different. It does not speak about what we do with our hands or our mouth. It speaks about what we desire in our heart.

It forbids:
- Desiring a better house than the one God has given us
- Desiring another person’s wife (or husband)
- Desiring another person’s servants, possessions, job, honour, abilities, or position
- Lusting after any woman on the road (every woman is someone’s daughter or wife)

No human being can keep this commandment by his own strength. It concerns the hidden desires of the heart.

> <div style="background-color:#F8D7DA; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #DC3545; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#721C24; font-weight:bold;">⚠️ Why God Gave the Tenth Commandment</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#491217;">
> • To reveal His true expectation — purity in the innermost being.<br>
> • To test our honesty.<br>
> • To show that even those who keep the first nine still fall short.<br>
> • To expose the dishonesty of anyone who claims to be perfect while still coveting.
> </span>
> </div>

The first nine can be kept for the approval of men. The tenth reveals whether we are content with an external testimony or whether we truly want to please God in secret.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ The Rich Young Ruler</span>

A rich ruler came to Jesus and asked, “What shall I do to inherit eternal life?” (Mark 10:17–22).

Jesus listed the outward commandments: honour your father and mother, do not murder, do not commit adultery, do not steal, do not bear false witness. He did not mention the tenth.

The ruler answered confidently, “Teacher, I have kept all these things from my youth.”

Jesus looked at him and loved him, then said, “One thing you lack: go and sell all you possess and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow Me.”

The ruler went away grieving, for he was very rich.

He loved money — the tenth commandment. He could have said, “Lord, I love money. I cannot keep the tenth commandment. Please help me.” Jesus would have helped him. Instead he remained rich and missed the opportunity to become an apostle like Peter.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Paul’s Honest Discovery</span>

Paul came from a good family. He was well educated and, according to the outward righteousness of the law, blameless (Philippians 3:6). He could say he had lived with a perfectly good conscience before men (Acts 23:1).

Yet when the tenth commandment spoke to him, it uncovered coveting of every kind:

> <span style="color:#276749;">“I would not have known about coveting if the Law had not said, ‘You shall not covet.’”</span>  
> <span style="color:#276749;">— Romans 7:7</span>

The commandment produced in him every type of coveting — for other people’s wives, daughters, money, honour, abilities, jobs, and positions. He cried out:

> <span style="color:#276749;">“Wretched man that I am! Who will set me free from the body of this death?”</span>  
> <span style="color:#276749;">— Romans 7:24</span>

The answer came only through Jesus Christ:

> <span style="color:#276749;">“For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and of death.”</span>  
> <span style="color:#276749;">— Romans 8:2</span>

God did what the law could not do (Romans 8:3).

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Jesus’ Example and God’s Desire</span>

Jesus lived without coveting. He did it in the power of the Holy Spirit. This proves that a pure heart is possible.

God desires “truth in the innermost being” (Psalm 51:6). He is not satisfied with external goodness that impresses men while the heart remains full of desire.

> <div style="background-color:#D1ECF1; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #17A2B8; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#0C5460; font-weight:bold;">💡 Old Covenant vs New Covenant</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#062C33;">
> The Old Testament law set an impossible standard and gave no power — like asking a person without legs to climb Everest or jump a twenty-foot wall. The New Covenant raises the standard still higher (a pure heart) but gives wings — the power of the Holy Spirit — so that we can fly.
> </span>
> </div>

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ How God Leads Us Into This Life</span>

God does not force this inward purity on us. He waits for honesty.

Many never enter because they will not acknowledge the corruption of their hearts. Paul did. He called himself a wretched man. Then God delivered him.

The way in is simple:
1. Admit: “Lord, I cannot keep the tenth commandment. I still covet.”
2. Ask for help.
3. Receive the grace and the power of the Holy Spirit.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Practical Application</span>

- Do not judge others by outward appearance. Man sees only about 10 percent; God sees the heart (the remaining 90 percent is hidden).
- Seek the Holy Spirit’s power for private victory over sin — the desires no one else can see.
- Do not settle for an external testimony that earns men’s approval. Desire truth in the innermost being.
- Claim the freedom promised in Romans 8:2.

---

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Key Takeaways</span>

> <div style="background-color:#D4EDDA; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #28A745; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#155724; font-weight:bold;">✅ Summary of Main Truths</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#0B3D1B;">
> • The first nine commandments deal mainly with outward actions that can be seen by others.<br>
> • The tenth commandment forbids coveting — any inward desire for what belongs to another.<br>
> • No one can keep the tenth commandment by human effort. It was given to expose the heart and test honesty.<br>
> • The rich young ruler kept the outward commandments but failed when money was touched. He refused to admit his need.<br>
> • Paul was outwardly blameless, yet the tenth commandment revealed every kind of coveting in him. He cried “Wretched man!”<br>
> • Through the Holy Spirit the law of the Spirit of life set Paul free from the law of sin and death (Romans 8:2).<br>
> • Jesus lived without coveting, proving a pure heart is possible by the Spirit’s power.<br>
> • God desires truth in the innermost being (Psalm 51:6).<br>
> • The New Covenant raises the standard (purity of heart) and gives the power (the Holy Spirit) to meet it.<br>
> • Honest confession of inward lust is the doorway to this deliverance. Do not settle for outward goodness.
> </span>
> </div>

Lord Jesus,  

I can keep some of the outward commandments, but my heart is not pure. I still covet — money, position, honour, abilities, and other things that belong to others.

I admit I cannot keep the tenth commandment by myself. I am a wretched man without You.

Please cleanse my heart and fill me with the power of the Holy Spirit. Set me free by the law of the Spirit of life from the law of sin and death. Give me truth in the innermost being.

Help me not to settle for an outward testimony that impresses people. I want to please You in private as well as in public. Make me radical in seeking Your power for inward purity.

In Your name, Amen.
