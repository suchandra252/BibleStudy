---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">1. The Root of Every Temptation</span>

Every temptation that comes to a human being is essentially the same as the one that came to Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden.

There were thousands of beautiful trees in the garden that God had freely given. There was only **one** tree that was forbidden.

When Adam and Eve stood before that tree, they were standing between two choices:

- The **Creator** (God Himself and His clear command), or  
- The **created** (a beautiful, attractive fruit that looked good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and desirable to make one wise).

<span style="background-color: #fef3c7; padding: 8px 12px; border-left: 4px solid #f59e0b; display: block; margin: 12px 0;">
<strong>Important Insight</strong><br>
The devil did not tempt them to murder, commit adultery, or do some “big” external sin. He simply tempted them to choose something <strong>created</strong> over the <strong>Creator</strong>. That was enough to destroy them and bring the entire human race into slavery.
</span>

---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">2. Why God Allowed the Forbidden Tree</span>

God is a God of free will. He does not force anyone to be holy.

If God had placed no restriction at all in the garden, Adam could never have become holy — because holiness requires **choice**. Without the possibility of saying “No” to something, there can be no true obedience or holiness.

God wanted sons and daughters who would choose Him freely out of love — not robots programmed to obey.

<span style="background-color: #ecfdf5; padding: 8px 12px; border-left: 4px solid #10b981; display: block; margin: 12px 0;">
<strong>Great Truth</strong><br>
Holiness is not the absence of temptation. Holiness is choosing the Creator over the created when the temptation comes.
</span>

---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">3. The Same Choice Confronts Us Every Day</span>

Today the form of the “forbidden fruit” has changed, but the choice remains exactly the same:

| Created Thing (the fruit) | The Creator |
|---------------------------|-------------|
| Money / material prosperity | God |
| Sexual lust / immoral pleasure | God |
| Honour, recognition, position | God |
| Comfort, ease, security | God |
| Even a loved relationship that competes with God | God |

Whenever we choose any of these created things above the clear will of God, we are repeating Adam’s sin.

<span style="background-color: #f0f9ff; padding: 8px 12px; border-left: 4px solid #3b82f6; display: block; margin: 12px 0;">
<strong>Result of the Wrong Choice</strong><br>
Man becomes a <strong>slave</strong> when he chooses created things over his Creator. Jesus came to deliver us from this slavery.
</span>

---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">4. Why God Delays Judgment</span>

God did not immediately strike Adam and Eve dead the moment they ate. He allowed them to continue living for many years.

Why?

So that we would learn to obey God **out of love**, not merely out of fear of immediate punishment.

If every sin was instantly punished, people would obey only from terror. But God desires a relationship of love and trust.

<span style="background-color: #fef3c7; padding: 8px 12px; border-left: 4px solid #f59e0b; display: block; margin: 12px 0;">
<strong>New Covenant Reality</strong><br>
Under the New Covenant, the Holy Spirit writes God’s laws on our hearts so that we delight to choose the Creator. Obedience flows from love, not from external fear.
</span>

---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">5. Characteristics of a Life That Chooses the Creator</span>

- A deep desire for God Himself more than for His gifts  
- Freedom from the love of money, honour, and comfort  
- Willingness to lose any created thing rather than lose fellowship with God  
- Daily, deliberate choices to put God first  
- Joy in simple obedience  

Jesus Himself lived this way perfectly. He never once chose any created thing above the Father.

---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">6. Key Spiritual Lessons</span>

- The origin of all human sin is choosing the created over the Creator.  
- God allows temptation so that we can freely choose holiness.  
- Every temptation we face is essentially the same test that Adam faced.  
- Choosing created things always leads to slavery.  
- God delays judgment so that we may learn to obey from love.  
- True discipleship means putting the Creator above every created person or thing — including the closest relationships (Luke 14:26).  
- Jesus came to set us free from the slavery of preferring the created.

---

## <span style="color: #0f766e; font-weight: bold;">7. Personal Application</span>

- In what areas of my life am I still choosing “beautiful fruit” (money, comfort, recognition, relationships) over the clear voice of God?  
- Do I obey mainly because I fear consequences, or because I love the Lord?  
- When I stand before a forbidden “tree” today, will I deliberately choose my Creator?  
- Am I willing to lose any created thing rather than lose intimacy with God?  
- Do I ask the Holy Spirit daily for strength to choose God above everything else?
