# **Matthew 1:1–10**
## **Bible Commentary Notes**
### *(Detailed Explanation based on Zac Poonen’s Verse-by-Verse Study)*

---

### **Matthew 1:1**
**“The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham.”**

This is the opening sentence of the entire New Testament. Matthew does not begin with a miracle, a parable, or even the birth of Jesus. He begins with a genealogy. This is deliberate and highly significant.

In the Old Testament, Genesis 5:1 records *“the book of the generations of Adam.”*  
Here, at the start of the New Testament, we have *“the book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ.”*

Scripture presents **two distinct lines or races**:

- The human race that descends from **Adam**
- The new, spiritual race that descends from **Jesus Christ**

Every human being is born into Adam’s line by natural birth. No one starts in Christ’s line. A person is transferred from Adam’s line into Christ’s line only through <u style="color: #1a5276;">**repentance and faith**</u>. This transfer is one of the most important truths in the whole Bible.

Matthew immediately presents Jesus with two titles that would have been very meaningful to a Jewish reader:

- **Son of David** – the promised King who would sit on David’s throne forever  
- **Son of Abraham** – the seed through whom God promised to bless all the families of the earth (Genesis 12:3)

By opening this way, Matthew is declaring from the first verse that Jesus is the long-awaited Messiah.

> <mark style="background-color: #fff3cd; padding: 4px 8px; border-left: 4px solid #f1c40f;">
> **Key Insight:** There are two books of generations — Adam’s (natural) and Christ’s (spiritual). Transfer happens only by repentance and faith.
> </mark>

---

### **Matthew 1:2–6a**
**The Line from Abraham to David**

Matthew traces the genealogy carefully through the patriarchs and the tribe of Judah down to King David. This section is not just a list of names. It establishes Jesus’ **legal right** to the throne of David.

This is **Joseph’s genealogy** (see verse 16). Joseph belonged to the royal line of David through Solomon. If a king had still been sitting on David’s throne in that generation, Joseph would have been the rightful heir. After Joseph, the legal right would have passed to Jesus as his foster son. This is why Matthew records Joseph’s line — to prove Jesus’ **legal claim** to the throne.

Luke’s Gospel records a different genealogy (Luke 3). That is the line of **Mary**. Jesus was physically *“born of the seed of David according to the flesh”* (Romans 1:3) through His mother Mary.

From Adam to David the two lines are the same. At David they divide:

- Joseph’s line continues through **Solomon** (the royal line)  
- Mary’s line continues through **Nathan** (another son of David)

Thus Jesus is both the **legal heir** (through Joseph) and the **physical descendant** (through Mary) of David. He truly took our flesh — the flesh of David.

Matthew begins with Abraham because he is writing primarily to the **Jews**.  
Luke begins with Adam because he is writing for a wider audience that includes Gentiles.

> <mark style="background-color: #d4edda; padding: 4px 8px; border-left: 4px solid #28a745;">
> **Important Truth:** Jesus is both the legal heir to David’s throne (through Joseph) and the physical seed of David (through Mary).
> </mark>

---

### **Matthew 1:3, 5–6**
**The Four Women – A Deliberate Choice**

Matthew does something very unusual. In Jewish genealogies, women were almost never mentioned. Matthew deliberately includes **four women**, and the women he chooses are striking:

#### **1. Tamar (verse 3)**
Genesis 38 records a shameful story. Tamar disguised herself as a prostitute and committed adultery with her own father-in-law, Judah. From that immoral union came Perez, who is listed in the genealogy of Jesus.  
<u style="color: #922b21;">Jesus chose to come through this line.</u>

#### **2. Rahab (verse 5)**
Rahab was the prostitute of Jericho (Joshua 2 and 6). She was a Gentile and a woman of immoral reputation. Later she married Salmon and became the mother of Boaz. She is also listed in the ancestry of Jesus.

#### **3. Ruth (verse 5)**
Ruth was a Moabitess. She was not an Israelite. The Moabites themselves originated from an incestuous relationship — Lot’s daughters committing adultery with their own father (Genesis 19). Ruth married Boaz and became the great-grandmother of King David. She too is included.

#### **4. Bathsheba (verse 6 – “the wife of Uriah”)**
Matthew does not even mention her name. He simply says *“the wife of Uriah.”* This points to the well-known story in 2 Samuel 11. David committed adultery with her and then arranged the murder of her husband Uriah. From that relationship (after Uriah’s death) came Solomon.

Every one of these four women is connected with some form of sexual immorality, shame, or questionable origin.

**Why did Jesus choose such a line?**  
Jesus is the only Person in history who could choose the family, the place, and the time of His birth. He could have selected the purest, most honourable, and most respected family line in Israel. Instead, He deliberately chose a family tree marked by adultery, prostitution, incest, and murder.

> <mark style="background-color: #f8d7da; padding: 4px 8px; border-left: 4px solid #dc3545;">
> **The Reason:** He “did not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.” He identified Himself with the lowest and the most broken so that no one would feel that their past was too dark for His grace.
> </mark>

### **Matthew 1:6b–10**
**The Kings from David to Josiah**

The genealogy continues through the kings of Judah: Solomon, Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa, Jehoshaphat, Joram, Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, Hezekiah, Manasseh, Amon, and Josiah.

Even in the royal line there is deep failure — idolatry, compromise, and judgment. The list is not a record of unbroken godliness. It is a record of <u style="color: #1a5276;">**God’s faithfulness in spite of human sin**</u>. God preserved the line of the Messiah even when the kings failed badly.

---

### **Main Lessons from This Passage**

1. **Two races exist**  
   Every person is born into Adam’s line. Only through repentance and faith is a person transferred into the line of Jesus Christ.

2. **Jesus has a dual claim to the throne**  
   He is both the legal heir (through Joseph) and the physical descendant (through Mary) of David.

3. **Grace reaches the lowest**  
   The deliberate inclusion of these four women shows that no past is too shameful for the grace of God. Jesus came for sinners.

4. **A strong call to humility**  
   Many people take pride in their family background, caste, education, or social standing. Jesus shatters all such pride. The One who could have chosen the highest place chose the lowest. True discipleship begins with the same humility.

5. **God’s faithfulness cannot be stopped**  
   Despite repeated failure in the royal line, God kept His promise and preserved the line of the Messiah.

> Jesus is the only One who could choose His family line — and He chose a line full of sinners so that every sinner could know they are welcome.
