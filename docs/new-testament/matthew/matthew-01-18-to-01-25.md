### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">📌 Matthew 1:18–19</span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">NASB Text</span>

> <span style="color:#922B21; font-size:1.05em;">
> 18 Now the birth of Jesus Christ was as follows: when His mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">before they came together</span> she was found to be with child <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">by the Holy Spirit</span>.<br>
> 19 And Joseph her husband, being a <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">righteous man</span> and not wanting to disgrace her, planned to send her away secretly.
> </span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">Joseph – A Righteous Man</span>

Joseph was a righteous man. Even though he was convinced in his own mind that Mary had been immoral, he did not want to disgrace her publicly.  

Under the law he had every right to expose her. Instead he planned to send her away secretly.  

He did not act impulsively. Verse 20 says he “considered this.” He thought carefully and almost certainly prayed. Because he was willing to wait and seek God, the Lord was able to speak to him.

> <div style="background-color:#FFF3CD; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #F1C40F; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#856404; font-weight:bold;">🔑 Key Insight</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#5D4E00;">
> The first person in the New Testament on whom the Holy Spirit came (Mary) experienced misunderstanding and reproach, not popularity.  
> Even those closest to us may not understand what the Holy Spirit is doing in our lives.  
> Only those who receive revelation from God (like Joseph) will understand.  
> This is a clear mark of the New Testament age: the Holy Spirit produces the character of Jesus, and that often brings misunderstanding rather than honour.
> </span>
> </div>

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">📌 Matthew 1:20–21</span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">NASB Text</span>

> <span style="color:#922B21; font-size:1.05em;">
> 20 But when he had considered this, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife; for the Child who has been conceived in her is of the Holy Spirit.<br>
> 21 She will bear a Son; and you shall call His name <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">Jesus</span>, for He will <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">save His people from their sins</span>.”
> </span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">The First Promise of the New Testament</span>

The angel gave Joseph two crucial pieces of information:

1. The Child is of the Holy Spirit.  
2. His name shall be **Jesus** (Saviour), because **He will save His people from their sins**.

This is the **first promise** recorded in the New Testament.  

It is very significant that the first promise is not “He will save His people from hell,” but “He will save His people from their sins.”  

Many people are more interested in being saved from hell than in being saved from sin. But the primary purpose of Jesus’ coming is to save us from sin itself — from lust, anger, bitterness, jealousy, love of money, and every other evil.

Forgiveness alone is not enough. A father who only says “I forgive you” to a child who has fallen into a pit, but does not pull him out, has not truly saved him. Jesus came both to forgive and to deliver us from the power of sin.

> <div style="background-color:#D4EDDA; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #28A745; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#155724; font-weight:bold;">✅ Important Truth</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#0B3D1B;">
> Salvation from sin is more than forgiveness of sins.  
> God can forgive us and yet we may continue to be defeated by sin day after day.  
> Jesus came to do both: to forgive us and to save us from the power of sin.  
> This is the first and foundational promise of the entire New Testament.
> </span>
> </div>

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">📌 Matthew 1:22–23</span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">NASB Text</span>

> <span style="color:#922B21; font-size:1.05em;">
> 22 Now all this took place to fulfill what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet:<br>
> 23 “Behold, the virgin shall be with child and shall bear a Son, and they shall call His name <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">Immanuel</span>,” which translated means, “<span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">God with us</span>.”
> </span>

This is the fulfilment of Isaiah 7:14.  

The name **Immanuel** means “God with us.” The eternal God stepped into human history and became one of us. This is the heart of the incarnation.

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">📌 Matthew 1:24–25</span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">NASB Text</span>

> <span style="color:#922B21; font-size:1.05em;">
> 24 And Joseph awoke from his sleep and did as the angel of the Lord commanded him, and took Mary as his wife,<br>
> 25 but kept her a virgin until she gave birth to a Son; and he called His name Jesus.
> </span>

Joseph’s response is a beautiful example of the **obedience of faith**.  

His own reason told him that Mary must have been unfaithful. Yet he set aside his reasoning and simply obeyed the word of God.  

He took a lifetime decision based on faith in what God had spoken, even though he could not fully understand it.  

This is the pattern of true New Testament obedience.

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Main Lessons from This Passage</span>

1. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">True righteousness protects others</span>  
   Joseph refused to use his “rights” to shame Mary. Righteousness is not only being right — it is choosing mercy.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 1:19 · Micah 6:8 · James 2:13</span>

2. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">The first promise of the New Testament is salvation from sin</span>  
   Jesus came primarily to save us from our sins, not merely from hell.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 1:21 · Titus 2:14 · Romans 6:14</span>

3. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">The Holy Spirit’s work often brings misunderstanding</span>  
   When the Spirit does a deep work, it may bring reproach rather than popularity — even from those closest to us.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 1:18 · John 15:18-20 · 2 Timothy 3:12</span>

4. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">Obedience of faith sets reason aside</span>  
   Joseph obeyed God’s word even when his own understanding could not grasp it. This is the obedience of faith.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 1:24 · Romans 1:5 · Hebrews 11:8</span>

5. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">God is with us</span>  
   The name Immanuel is the greatest comfort: the eternal God has come to dwell with His people.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 1:23 · Isaiah 7:14 · John 1:14</span>

> <span style="color:#002752;">
> The first page of the New Testament already shows us the heart of the gospel:  
> A righteous man who chooses mercy,  
> A virgin who carries the Son of God,  
> A Saviour whose primary mission is to save us from our sins,  
> And a God who has come to be with us.  
> Everything that follows in the New Testament flows from these truths.
