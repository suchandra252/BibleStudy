### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">📌 Matthew 4:1–2</span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">NASB Text</span>

> <span style="color:#922B21; font-size:1.05em;">
> 1 Then Jesus was <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">led up by the Spirit</span> into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil.<br>
> 2 And after He had fasted forty days and forty nights, He then became hungry.
> </span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">Led by the Spirit into Temptation</span>

Immediately after the Spirit came upon Jesus at His baptism and the Father declared Him to be His beloved Son, the same Spirit led Him into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil.

This is significant. Right after a high spiritual experience, temptation often follows. We see the same pattern in Genesis: immediately after God created man and woman and united them, the devil came to tempt them. Adam failed in a pleasant garden. Jesus, the second Man (the last Adam), was tempted in a harsh wilderness after forty days of fasting.

Luke 4:2 tells us that Jesus was tempted by the devil throughout the forty days. What Matthew records are the final three intense temptations at the end of that period. God does not Himself tempt anyone (James 1:13), but He permits temptation for a good purpose — so that Jesus, having been tempted in every point like us, can fully sympathize with our weaknesses (Hebrews 4:15).

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">The Purpose of the Wilderness</span>

The wilderness experience came right after the anointing and the Father’s approval. Higher degrees of faithfulness bring higher degrees of temptation. Jesus had already overcome temptation for thirty years in private life. Now He faced a greater test as He prepared to begin public ministry.

> <div style="background-color:#FFF3CD; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #F1C40F; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#856404; font-weight:bold;">🔑 Key Insight</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#5D4E00;">
> • Immediately after the Spirit’s anointing and the Father’s approval, the Spirit led Jesus into temptation.<br>
> • Adam failed in a pleasant garden; Jesus overcame in a harsh wilderness after forty days of fasting.<br>
> • God permits temptation for a purpose — so that Jesus can fully sympathize with us.<br>
> • Higher faithfulness brings higher degrees of temptation.
> </span>
> </div>

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">📌 Matthew 4:3–4</span>

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">NASB Text</span>

> <span style="color:#922B21; font-size:1.05em;">
> 3 And the tempter came and said to Him, “If You are the Son of God, command that these stones become bread.”<br>
> 4 But He answered and said, “It is written, ‘Man shall not live on bread alone, but on <span style="background-color:#FFE4E1; padding:1px 4px; border-radius:3px;">every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God</span>.’”
> </span>

<img src="https://media.freebibleimages.org/stories/FB_GNPI_014_Jesus_Temptation/source-jpeg/03_FB_GNPI_014_Jesus_Temptation_1024.jpg?1725894120" width="500">

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">The First Temptation – Doubt and Self-Interest</span>

After forty days of fasting, Jesus was hungry. The devil came with a twofold attack.

First, he tried to make Jesus doubt what God had just said. God had declared, “This is My beloved Son.” The devil began with “If You are the Son of God…” The first thing the devil often does is to make us question what God has clearly spoken — especially our identity as God’s children.

Second, the devil tempted Jesus to use the power He had just received (the anointing of the Spirit) for His own personal need — turning stones into bread to satisfy hunger. The power of God is never to be used for self. Jesus later multiplied bread for five thousand others, but He refused to use that power for Himself.

#### <span style="color:#6C3483;">Living by Every Word of God</span>

Jesus answered as a man, not by claiming His rights as the Son of God. He had emptied Himself (Philippians 2:7) and took His stand as a human being. He quoted Deuteronomy 8:3: “Man shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God.”

Physical bread is necessary, but it is not the most important thing. The primary need of man is the word that comes from God’s mouth. Spiritual life is sustained by daily receiving and obeying God’s Word. This keeps our sense of values right and protects us from materialism and the misuse of spiritual gifts for personal gain.

> <div style="background-color:#D4EDDA; padding:12px 16px; border-left:5px solid #28A745; border-radius:5px; margin:15px 0;">
> <span style="color:#155724; font-weight:bold;">✅ Important Truth</span><br><br>
> <span style="color:#0B3D1B;">
> • The devil’s first attack is often to make us doubt what God has clearly said about our identity.<br>
> • The power of the Spirit is never to be used for self — only for God’s glory and the benefit of others.<br>
> • Jesus refused to meet a legitimate physical need by using divine power for Himself.<br>
> • Man’s primary need is not bread, but every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.<br>
> • Jesus overcame as a man, taking His stand on the written Word, not on His divine privileges.
> </span>
> </div>

### <span style="color:#1A5276; background-color:#EAF2F8; padding:5px 12px; border-radius:6px;">✨ Main Lessons from This Passage</span>

1. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">Temptation often follows spiritual high points</span>  
   After the Spirit’s anointing and the Father’s approval, the Spirit Himself led Jesus into the wilderness.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 4:1 · James 1:2-3 · 1 Peter 4:12</span>

2. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">Jesus overcame where the first Adam failed</span>  
   Adam fell in a garden; Jesus triumphed in the wilderness after extreme hunger.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 4:1-2 · Romans 5:19 · 1 Corinthians 15:45</span>

3. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">Never use God’s power for self</span>  
   Spiritual gifts and anointing are for God’s glory and the blessing of others, never for personal advantage.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 4:3 · 1 Corinthians 10:24 · Philippians 2:4</span>

4. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">Live by every word of God</span>  
   Physical needs are real, but the Word of God is the true source of life.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 4:4 · Deuteronomy 8:3 · Job 23:12</span>

5. <span style="color:#6C3483; font-weight:bold;">Jesus overcame as a man</span>  
   He did not rely on His divine privileges but took His stand on the written Word as a human being.  
   <span style="color:#1A5276; font-size:0.95em;">→ Matthew 4:4 · Philippians 2:5-8 · Hebrews 4:15</span>

> refuse to doubt what God has spoken,  
> refuse to use spiritual power for self,  
> and live by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.  
> This is how the last Adam overcame where the first Adam failed —  
> and this is the same path of victory available to every believer.
> </span>
